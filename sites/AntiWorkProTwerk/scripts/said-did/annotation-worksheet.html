What data am I looking at?

A case is one stretch of text assigned to a speaker—not a whole speech, a whole bill, or an accusation. Several cases may come from the same person or debate. Some contain only parliamentary business and no usable position. Those are useful tests too.

The Congressional Record is Congress’s written record. It can contain inserted or quoted material; it is not proof that every word was spoken aloud. A roll call records individual votes. A bill version is a particular draft of proposed legislation.

These are archived records, not a live feed. Collection code proposed speaker names and text boundaries; it can be wrong. The action inventory covers only the people collected for this pilot—not every vote or every member of Congress.

What should the result be?

A saved answer sheet saying, in your judgment:

  • Who owns these words, and what the complete passage is.
  • What position or commitment it expresses, if any.
  • Whether a recorded action is relevant, and what comparison the evidence permits.

The download is a data file the local evaluator can read. You don’t need to write code or edit JSON.

What are you not being asked to do?

  • Decide whether you like the politician or policy.
  • Prove that someone lied or guess their private motives.
  • Assume a later vote contradicts an earlier statement.
  • Approve a public finding or finish every case in one sitting.

“I can’t tell” is a valid stopping point. Leave a note and keep the case pending.

A made-up example: what a good review looks like

Fictional statement: “I’ll vote for the Clean River Bill if it includes funding for small towns.”

Your reading: an intention to vote in support, with the exact condition “if it includes funding for small towns.” The tone is separate from the stance.

Fictional action: the same person later votes No. That alone is not enough to call it a contradiction. Did the voted-on version include the funding? Was it actually a final vote on that bill?

Defensible result if those facts are missing: more context is needed. This example supplies no answers for the real cases below.

First read the text. You can pause before making any judgment. Luna’s predictions are intentionally hidden to avoid influencing you.
