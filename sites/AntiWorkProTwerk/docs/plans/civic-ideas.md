**The strongest direction is to reveal something specific and checkable—not have AI assign people a “corruption score.”** Think: *Who requested this exception? What changed inside this bill? How does my retirement fund vote? Where did this government contract’s money go?*

Here are **40 U.S.-focused ideas with free data sources**. The descriptions are proposed product capabilities, not claims that misconduct has already been found. **★ marks the five I would investigate first.**

## What “free data” does—and does not—mean

**The most straightforward starting sources are genuinely machine-readable.** USAspending’s endpoints require no authorization; SEC submissions and company-facts APIs require no API key; EPA offers downloadable datasets; and LegiScan has a capped free API tier. That removes the need to buy a commercial data feed, but not the work of cleaning and matching records. ([USAspending API][14])

**Freshness varies.** For example, 13F filings can arrive up to 45 days after quarter-end. An honest investment app should show both the holdings date and disclosure date, rather than advertise these as current trades. ([Investor.gov][32])

**Public access is not unrestricted reuse.** FEC individual-contributor information has commercial-use and solicitation restrictions, with particular treatment for journalistic uses. GDELT’s availability also does not give you ownership of publishers’ articles. Build around permitted analysis, citations, and original records—not donor lead lists or republished article archives. ([FEC.gov][33])

**My strongest recommendation:** start with **Who Votes Your Retirement?** for a consumer-facing product, or **Bill Diff** for a democracy-focused product. Both can deliver a useful, verifiable answer immediately—even when the answer is ordinary rather than scandalous.
